Operations Reference

How ZeeFashion Actually Works

Everything the app and the admin panel do, end to end — from the moment someone signs up, through every way there is to buy something, to the moment money actually lands in a bank account. Written from the real code, not the marketing description.

3 separate wallets per person
6 distinct ways to buy something
2 referral systems running side by side
25 admin control-panel pages
1 external logistics partner (Eziza)
Getting In

Getting Started

Every account starts as a buyer. Becoming a merchant, an influencer, or anything else is a deliberate second step, not a signup choice.

Onboarding & Sign Up

First launch shows three full-screen slides — shopping, buying together, and custom orders — as a one-time introduction. Once you've seen them, they never show again.

Sign-up is a single form with no role picker — name, phone, email, password. Every new account is a buyer. There's one optional field, collapsed by default: "Add referral code." Typing a code checks it live against two completely different systems at once and shows an inline badge before you even submit:

What you typed matches…Badge shownWhat actually happens
An onboarding officer's codePartnerYou're linked to that officer — relevant if you go on to become a merchant (see Growth & Referrals)
Another user's personal codeUserThat person earns a referral credit the first time you (or a merchant you later become) transact
NeitherInvalidNothing — you can still sign up, just without a referral link

Login, forgot-password, and reset-password all follow standard email flows.

Becoming a Merchant

There's no "switch to selling" toggle. A buyer submits a Seller Application — store name, business type, location, phone — and an admin approves or rejects it from the admin panel (a rejection requires a written reason). You're notified either way, and only on approval do the store-management tools in the app unlock.

Shopping

Shopping & Discovery

Browsing, searching, reviewing, and wishlisting — the everyday layer everything else sits on top of.

Search — Two Different Ways to Find Something

Typing into search actually runs one of two engines, and you're never told which one answered you:

ModeWhat it can do
Keyword searchMatches the literal words you typed against a product's name, description, and brand. "Red gown" only finds products that literally contain those words.
Semantic searchUnderstands meaning. "Red gown" can surface a product titled "Crimson Evening Dress" — it never sees the word "red," but the meaning matches.
What you'll see

The "understands meaning" search quietly switches back to plain keyword search whenever it can't run — most often when the AI service behind it has hit its usage limit for the moment. Nothing looks broken and no error appears; search just becomes literal for a while instead of meaning-based. And once the app has looked up what a phrase means, it remembers the answer, so scrolling down for more results never has to ask twice.

Reviews

Buyers rate and review products they've bought. Two things go beyond a plain star rating: merchants can publicly reply to any review on their own product, and other shoppers can mark a review "Helpful," with a running count shown next to it.

Wishlist & Gifting a Wishlist Item

A shopper builds a collection of saved products and can share it via a public link. Anyone with that link can buy an item for the list's owner. The twist: the resulting cashback credit goes to the list owner, not to the friend who actually paid — buying someone a gift also hands them a small reward for having wanted it. The rate structure mirrors Buy Together's exactly, at these live settings:

SettingLive value
Standard rate5%
New-recipient rate (your first time ever receiving a wishlist gift)10%
Minimum purchase to qualify₦100
Cap — same commission-share ceiling as Buy Together60%
Worked example

A friend buys you a ₦4,000 bag from your public wishlist. At the standard 5% rate that's ₦200 — but here the cap actually bites: the platform's own commission on a ₦4,000 sale (at a typical 4.5% rate) is only ₦180, and 60% of that is ₦108. So instead of ₦200, you receive ₦108, capped down to protect the platform's own margin on a small sale. On a bigger gift the cap matters much less — see the Buy Together example above, where it doesn't bind at all.

Every product detail view also quietly increments a view counter, visible to the merchant as basic interest data on their own listing.

Shopping

Merchant Tools

What a store owner actually manages once their seller application is approved.

Products

Merchants add and edit products with variants (size, colour). Deleting a product never actually deletes the row — it flips a hidden flag instead. Too many other things point at a product by its ID (past orders, combos, influencer stats), so a real delete would break all of them; a "deleted" product simply stops appearing anywhere new.

Merchant Goals

A private, self-set monthly target — an orders count and a revenue figure the merchant sets for themselves — tracked against what actually happened that month. It's purely a personal dashboard: it doesn't unlock anything and doesn't gate anything, it's just a way for a merchant to watch their own progress.

Getting Paid

A merchant's money sits in two entirely separate pots (see Money Movement for the full wallet picture): ordinary sales revenue, and gift income from fans. Each is requested as its own payout — the merchant submits a request, an admin approves it, and Paystack sends the actual bank transfer.

Ways to Buy

Group Buying & Bundles

Three ways to buy something other than "add to cart and check out" — together with others, over time by yourself, or as a bundle someone else assembled.

Buy Together (Pool)

Any buyer can start a pool on a product. The target is simply the product's full price — it isn't divided by however many people you expect to join. Anyone with the link can contribute any amount, by wallet or by Paystack, as many times as they like, until the pool's total reaches the target. The moment it does, an order is placed automatically, under the pool's initiator.

  • Start a pool — pick a product, size/colour and delivery address lock in immediately
  • Others contribute — wallet or Paystack, any amount, any number of times
  • Target reached — order auto-places under the initiator's account, everyone gets notified

    • Two things can happen to the target price while a pool is still open, and they're handled very differently:

    What happensEffect on the pool
    Merchant drops the priceEvery contributor gets a proportional refund back to their wallet, and the target itself is lowered to match
    Merchant raises the priceThe target simply rises — no refunds, contributors just need to close a slightly bigger gap
    Product goes out of stockEvery contributor is refunded in full
    Initiator cancelsEvery contributor is refunded in full
    Can't accidentally pay twice

    If you tap "Contribute" twice in a row by mistake, the second tap is blocked for two minutes so you can't accidentally pay into the same pool twice. It's still perfectly fine to make two genuinely separate contributions of the same amount a few minutes apart — the app is careful to only block true accidental double-taps, not real repeat support.

    Cashback goes only to the pool's initiator, never to contributors — a reward for organizing the pool, not for joining one. It's calculated as a percentage of what the initiator themselves put in (not the whole pool), at the platform's current live settings:

    SettingLive value
    Standard rate (returning initiators)5%
    New-initiator rate (your very first pool)10%
    Minimum contribution to qualify₦1,000
    Cap — cashback can never exceed this share of the platform's own commission on the order60%
    Worked example

    You start a pool for a ₦20,000 dress and personally contribute ₦5,000 of it (friends cover the other ₦15,000). At the standard 5% rate, you'd earn ₦250 cashback. If this is your very first pool, the 10% new-initiator rate applies instead — ₦500. Either way it's checked against the cap: the platform's own commission on a ₦20,000 order (at a typical 4.5% merchant rate) is ₦900, and 60% of that is ₦540 — so both the ₦250 and the ₦500 outcomes are paid in full, since neither reaches the ₦540 ceiling.

    Cashback expires 14 days after being earned unless converted into a one-time discount coupon first, and that coupon then expires 24 hours after conversion.

    Pools are shareable via a deep link that opens the app directly if it's installed, or a plain web page with an "Open in App" button if it isn't.

    Save to Buy

    The one-person version of the same idea: save toward a single product at your own pace instead of splitting it with anyone. The price is locked in the moment the goal is created, so a later price change on the product doesn't move your target. Deposit any amount, any number of times, by wallet or Paystack.

    SituationWhat happens
    Saved amount reaches the targetOrder places automatically. If the very last deposit pushed you past the target (say the price dropped in the meantime), the extra is refunded to your wallet automatically
    You withdraw / cancelFull refund of everything saved so far, no questions asked
    Product goes out of stockSaving is not blocked — you can keep depositing, and you're notified the moment it restocks
    Merchant discontinues the product entirelyThe goal is automatically cancelled and everything saved is refunded
    Goal sits unfunded too longA daily housekeeping job expires stale goals and refunds whatever was saved — goals currently expire after 90 days, with a reminder nudge at the 7-day-left mark and, if the item is out of stock, a further nudge every 30 days
    No cashback here

    Unlike Buy Together and wishlist gifting below, Save to Buy pays no bonus on top. Withdrawing or completing a goal only ever returns exactly what was saved (plus any accidental overshoot) — there's nothing extra to earn by saving toward something alone.

    Combo Purchase

    Two completely different origins feed the same "buy a bundle" experience:

    Merchant combos — a merchant bundles their own products together with a discount of up to 80%. Every new combo starts as a draft and needs admin approval before it goes live.

    Platform auto-combos — regenerated every day, four different kinds: products from the same merchant, a shared price range, the same category, or items the system has learned are complementary.

    Buying a combo is a single purchase with one confirmation reference — even when the bundle spans multiple different merchants. The buyer gets one email; each merchant involved gets their own separate notification about their part of the sale. If any item in a combo is out of stock, that specific combo simply can't be bought until it's back. The home-feed carousel deliberately caps at two combos from any one merchant at a time, so the feed doesn't end up dominated by a single shop.

    Ways to Buy

    Custom Fit

    A bidding marketplace for made-to-order and tailored pieces — closer to a freelance job board than a product listing.

  • Customer posts a request — title, description, reference photos, and their own bidding deadline (anywhere from 6 hours to 30 days out; 2 days is the suggested default)
  • Artisans browse and bid — any merchant meeting the request's own eligibility gates, if it has any
  • Back-and-forth — the customer can counter an offer; the artisan can accept the counter, or decline it and let their original bid stand
  • Customer accepts a bid — an order is created, with its own order number so it's easy to look up later (something like CUSTOMFIT-482-19)

    • A customer can optionally lock a request down with up to four requirements — an artisan who doesn't meet one of them simply can't bid at all:

    RequirementWhat it checks
    LocationThe artisan's store location must match the request's state or city
    Sales historyA minimum number of completed, paid orders
    RatingA minimum average star rating across all their products
    Subscription tierA minimum plan level — e.g. "Standard or above only"

    A separate, always-on check compares the customer's maximum acceptable delivery time against what each bid promises — a bid promising a longer turnaround than the customer will accept is rejected before it can even be submitted.

    The bid fee

    Placing a bid costs the artisan an admin-configurable fee — ₦500 by default — deducted from their wallet the instant they bid. It is not refunded under any circumstance: not if the customer picks someone else, not if the artisan withdraws, not if the request expires unanswered. It functions as a straightforward pay-to-bid fee.

    Once a bid is accepted, the platform also takes a separate cut of the job itself: a percentage of the labor portion of the price, and a separate percentage of the fabric cost portion — both figures set independently by an admin.

    A request that passes its own bidding deadline unanswered expires automatically (checked hourly), and any bids still sitting pending on it are rejected along with it.

    Getting It To You

    Fulfillment & Logistics

    From "the merchant marked it delivered" to "the courier is on the map right now" — including a full external delivery partner working behind the scenes.

    Two Different Kinds of "Status"

    Every order actually tracks two separate things, and confusing them is the easiest way to misread the system:

    Order status — is this a genuine, completed transaction, or was it cancelled? A simple yes/no on the money side.

    Delivery status — where the physical package actually is right now: Order Placed → Order Shipped → Out For Delivery → Awaiting Confirmation → Delivered

    The rule that protects buyers

    When a merchant marks a delivery as done, the system deliberately does not set it to "Delivered" — it goes to "Awaiting Confirmation" instead. Only the buyer's own "Confirm Receipt" tap ever sets the real Delivered status, and that single action is what unlocks the merchant's ability to request payout on that order. A merchant can never confirm their own payout by themselves.

    Internal Logistics — Riders & Companies Bidding

    Independent riders and logistics companies apply and are approved by an admin before they can carry anything. A rider is bound to a single coverage state; a company, running a fleet, can cover several. When a merchant marks their side of an order "Ready for Pickup," a bidding round opens to nearby riders and companies, and the buyer watches offers come in live — price, rating, distance — choosing whichever one they like and paying the delivery fee separately from the product price itself (same wallet/Paystack flow, same PIN confirmation as any other payment).

    A delivery moves through six plain stages, each one requiring a real action from a real person before it can advance to the next:

    Bidding Open→Rider Assigned→Rider at the Store→Picked Up→Delivered→Confirmed by Buyer

    The rider marks their own arrival at the store; the merchant then confirms the physical handoff, which is what actually moves things to "Picked Up." The rider marks arrival at the buyer; only the buyer's own confirmation closes the loop and releases the rider's earnings. The buyer can then leave a 1–5 star rating, which feeds the average shown on that rider's future bids.

    Eziza — the External Logistics Partner

    ZeeFashion can hand a delivery off to Eziza, a fully separate logistics company with its own app and its own riders, instead of using its own internal marketplace. This handoff happens quietly behind the scenes, arranged directly between ZeeFashion and Eziza — nothing about it is visible or exposed on the buyer's or merchant's phone. To a buyer or merchant, the experience is built to look and feel identical either way: the same live map, the same incoming-bids list, the same six stages above — it's simply a different set of riders bidding behind the scenes.

    Instant Courier (External Carriers)

    For deliveries handed off to Eziza, a buyer doesn't have to wait for a rider to bid at all. They can request real quotes from courier partners (DHL, GIG, and others) and book + pay for pickup immediately — shown as "Instant Courier Booked" rather than a rider assignment. This option is built and ready, but it's still waiting on the courier partner's own approval before it can be tested against real orders, so its pricing hasn't been proven out with a live delivery yet.

    Money

    Money Movement

    Every person on ZeeFashion actually holds up to three separate balances, each with a different job.

    WalletWhat lives in it
    Main balanceEveryday spending money — top-ups, sales revenue, refunds. This is what pays for a purchase.
    Gift walletA merchant's tip income only, shown as its own gold "Gift Earnings" card — kept visually and functionally separate from ordinary sales revenue.
    Referral walletPeer-referral earnings only. Not spendable in the app at all — it can only be withdrawn.

    Topping Up & Paying

    Topping up opens a Paystack payment screen right inside the app. Your balance doesn't go up just because the checkout screen says "success," though — Paystack separately tells ZeeFashion's own systems that the payment really went through, and it's that confirmation, not the checkout screen, that actually adds the money to your balance.

    Confirming an actual purchase asks for your PIN, or, if you've turned it on, your device's fingerprint or face — either is accepted as proof it's really you.

    Reading Your Transaction History

    Every entry in your history has a plain description telling you exactly what happened, plus a short internal label most people never need to think about. Here's what each label actually means, in case you ever see one and wonder:

    Money addedA top-up, or a direct credit to your balancecredit · deposit
    A fan tipped youGift income, for merchantsgift_received
    Money returned to youFrom a cancelled order, a Save to Buy goal, or a Buy Together poolrefunded · stb_refund · pool_refund
    Money taken outA withdrawal, or a payout you requesteddebit · withdrawal
    You tipped a merchantSending a gift from a product or store pagegift_sent
    A payout was actually paidTo a merchant, officer, or influencerpayout
    You joined a poolPaid into someone's Buy Together poolpool_contribution
    Custom Fit bid feeThe ₦500 fee artisans pay to place a bidbid_fee
    Custom Fit purchasePaid for an accepted Custom Fit ordercustom_purchase

    Gifts — Tipping a Merchant

    Gifting only ever flows buyer → merchant, sent straight from a product page or a store's profile — there's no buyer-to-buyer gifting. Each gift item has its own price and its own split between the merchant and ZeeFashion, set individually per gift rather than one flat rate for all of them. The moment you tap a gift, its price is set aside from your balance right away — even before the app has finished confirming the payment — so tapping twice by accident can never spend more than you actually have. The merchant is notified straight away with your name, the gift, and any message you attached, and sees a running feed and total count of gifts on their own dashboard.

    Money

    Subscriptions & Plans

    What a merchant actually pays for, and what really happens when they outgrow their plan.

    Plans — Starter and up — are set up and changed entirely by an admin: name, monthly price, a separate annual price, an order cap, and a commission percentage. None of this is fixed permanently into the app, so a new plan can be introduced at any time without updating the app itself.

    Two things actually differ between tiers, not just in name:

    LeverWhat it means in practice
    Monthly order capHow many orders a merchant can take before hitting their limit. The top tier implements "unlimited" simply as a cap of zero, meaning no cap at all.
    Commission %ZeeFashion's cut of each sale on that plan. This rate is locked onto every order the instant it's placed, so a later upgrade or downgrade never rewrites the commission on an order that's already happened.
    Hitting your cap doesn't block the sale

    A customer's payment always goes through, cap or no cap. If the merchant is over their monthly limit, the order is placed but stays completely invisible to them, with a 24-hour countdown attached. Upgrade within that window, and everything appears exactly as if it had always been visible. Don't upgrade in time, and the customer is automatically refunded and the order is cancelled — the merchant's plan status genuinely decides whether that sale survives.

    An admin can also hand a merchant a plan directly, entirely outside Paystack's billing cycle — a manual plan. These get a warning 7 days before they lapse, another at 1 day, and then automatically drop back down to Starter if nothing happens in between.

    Growth Engine

    Growth & Referrals

    Four different ways ZeeFashion grows — one that's automatic for every user, one that pays a small internal sales team, one for shop-scoped discount codes, and one for social-media promotion.

    Peer Referrals — Automatic for Everyone

    Every account gets its own referral code — something like ZEE-7F3K9A — the moment it's created, with nothing to set up or apply for. Share it, and you earn a flat ₦ amount every time the person you referred does something that counts. Live settings, right now:

    SettingLive value
    You earn, per order, when your referral buys something as a shopper₦200
    You earn, per order, when your referral (as a merchant) makes a sale₦200
    Minimum order size for either to countnone
    Maximum number of paying orders from the same referred person5
    Worked example

    You refer a friend using your code. They go on to place 5 separate orders as a shopper — you earn ₦200 each time, for ₦1,000 total. From their 6th order onward, that same relationship has hit its cap and earns you nothing further.

    Everything earned lands in the separate referral wallet described above, withdrawable on request.

    Onboarding Officers — Not Self-Service

    This is a completely different system that happens to share the same signup field. Onboarding officers aren't applicants — they're ZeeFashion's own field agents, created directly by an admin in the admin panel with their own referral code. A merchant who signs up under an officer's code generates a commission for that officer on every qualifying sale afterward — a percentage of ZeeFashion's own commission, not the sale itself. Live settings, right now:

    SettingLive value
    Platform's own commission rate (what this is a percentage of)4.5%
    Officer's share of that commission10%
    Cap — officer's share can never exceed this much of the platform's commission30%
    Minimum order size to qualify₦5,000
    Max qualifying orders per merchant, per month5
    Max qualifying orders per merchant, lifetime5
    Worked example

    A merchant who signed up under Officer Ada's code sells a ₦20,000 order. The platform's own commission on it is 4.5% × ₦20,000 = ₦900. Officer Ada's raw share is 10% of that ₦900 = ₦90. That's checked against the cap — 30% of ₦900 is ₦270 — and since ₦90 is well under ₦270, Officer Ada is credited the full ₦90. A ₦3,000 order from the same merchant wouldn't qualify at all (below the ₦5,000 minimum), and once that merchant has generated 5 qualifying orders in one calendar month, any further orders that month earn Officer Ada nothing more.

    An order where a platform-funded coupon was used is deliberately excluded from this calculation entirely, so an officer is never paid a commission on a sale the platform already subsidized.

    Coupons

    Two separate kinds of coupon exist, working the same way at checkout but created very differently:

    Merchant coupons — created by a merchant for their own products (percentage or flat ₦ off, minimum order, usage cap, optional expiry, optionally scoped to specific products). The merchant always covers 100% of their own coupon's discount.

    Platform coupons — created by an admin, aimable at everyone, specific products, specific merchants, or specific customers, and configurable to split the discount's cost between the platform and the affected merchant.

    Either way, a shopper can use the exact same coupon code only once, ever. And a coupon code can never collide with an influencer code — the system blocks that at creation time, so typing a code is never ambiguous about which discount system it belongs to.

    Influencers

    Anyone with at least 100 followers on a supported platform can apply; an admin approves or rejects the application. Approval alone doesn't make an influencer's code work everywhere — it only activates for a specific merchant once that merchant has explicitly authorized that influencer (optionally limited to specific products), and only if the merchant allows influencer promotion at all.

    The commission an influencer earns, and the discount their code gives shoppers, is decided by whichever of these has been set, checked in this order: a special rate for that one merchant, if one was ever agreed — otherwise the influencer's own personal rate — otherwise just the plain default rate for their tier. Earnings sit as "pending" after each sale, and rather than choosing an amount to withdraw, an influencer requests payment of everything owed at once.

    Growth Engine

    Community & Rewards

    Badges, streaks, and the leaderboard — the layer that makes routine activity feel like it's being noticed.

    Badges & Streaks

    Every badge is defined as data — a name, an emoji, who it's for, and the exact count that unlocks it — so new tiers can be added by an admin without touching the app itself. The system tracks three kinds of milestone:

    KindWhat triggers it
    Buyer badgesTotal completed orders (roughly tiered around 1 / 5 / 20 / 50 / 100 — exact live thresholds are admin-configurable and can be changed at any time)
    Merchant badgesTotal completed sales (roughly tiered around 1 / 5 / 50 / 100 / 500, same caveat)
    Weekly streaksBeing active in consecutive Monday-starting weeks — any gap resets the current streak to 1, though your longest-ever streak is remembered permanently

    A brand-new badge plays a one-time celebration animation the first time it's seen.

    Leaderboard

    Two public boards — buyers and merchants — each viewable across three windows: this week, this month, and all-time. The top 50 are shown to everyone; if you're not in that top group, you still get a personal "Your Rank" card so you know roughly where you stand.

    Trust & Support

    Trust & Safety

    Three genuinely different channels exist for "something's wrong" — a formal dispute process, a general support line, and ordinary conversation. They are not the same thing, and don't share a screen.

    Disputes

    Either side of an order — buyer or merchant — can raise a formal dispute, choosing from seven categories: Not as Described, Damaged/Defective, Not Delivered, Wrong Item, Refund Not Processed, Late Delivery, or Other. A dispute starts as Just Opened, moves to Admin Reviewing once an admin steps in and privately asks the other side to respond, then closes as either Resolved or Rejected.

    Evidence stays private the whole way through — each party can only see their own submission, and only the admin ever sees both sides. There are deliberately only three possible outcomes, to keep the maths simple and consistent:

    OutcomeWhat actually happens
    Full refundAuto-calculated and credited straight to the buyer's wallet — no partial refunds exist, specifically to avoid inconsistent commission/cashback maths
    ReplacementA label only. No money moves and no new order is created — the merchant coordinates the actual replacement with the buyer over in-app chat
    DismissedNo action; the dispute is simply closed

    Support Tickets

    A separate, general-purpose line straight to the platform itself — not tied to a specific order the way a dispute is, and not a conversation with a merchant the way chat is. A customer opens a ticket under one of eight categories (Order, Payment, Account, Product, Delivery, Refund, Technical, or Other Issue), and an admin replies directly from the admin panel, triggering a push notification the moment they do.

    In-App Chat

    A dedicated conversation is created automatically for every single order, between that buyer and that merchant — nothing to set up. It comes with a collapsible product-context banner, a shared measurements sheet either side can fill in or edit, image messages, and real double-tick read receipts so you know the second the other person has actually seen a message.

    Being an admin is simply a switch flipped on someone's ordinary account — there's no separate admin login system to manage. And even though the panel runs in an ordinary web browser rather than through a specially locked-down system, it's built so it can only ever see or change exactly what an admin is meant to touch, nothing more.

    This reference describes ZeeFashion as it actually behaves today, checked directly against the app and the admin panel — not against marketing copy or an old feature list. Where a figure can be changed by an admin (bid fees, referral amounts, commission splits, badge thresholds), the number shown is today's setting, not a fixed rule — an admin can adjust any of them at any time.
